A stale notification from a finished workflow turned out to be carrying work. The tail of the previous authoring run had rewritten eighteen shards after the first seventy questions were merged and shipped, leaving forty-five more on disk that no build had ever seen. Collecting them, and levelling the five subjects they left shallow, took the pool from ninety-four to a hundred and sixty-four.

Then measuring what a campaign actually sees turned up something worse, and something already shipped: the size of the pool was barely relevant. Over two hundred sessions one subject reached two of its twelve questions, and a single question came up eleven times.

A hash of the turn is not variety

The defect

Selection deliberately spends no randomness. v8EnsureQuestion runs on the render path — every action, every tab change — so a die there would make a campaign's dice-spend depend on how often the player looked at a tab. The original answer was a hash of the session number:

h = turn × 2654435761, folded over the subject's characters, then h mod n

That looks like variety and is not. For a fixed subject the value moves by a constant stride every session. An arithmetic progression taken modulo n visits only n ÷ gcd(stride, n) of the residues — so wherever the stride shared a factor with the number of questions on that subject, the campaign landed on the same few for ever, and no amount of authoring could reach the rest.

The controls below are the whole mechanism. Sixty sessions, a shelf of n questions:

Set the shelf to twelve and the stride to six and the shelf collapses to two questions, whatever else is written on it. That is not a contrived setting: it is what the real hash did to the promise subject.

Count what has been asked

The fix

The item is no longer chosen by hash at all. Each subject keeps a count of how many times it has been raised, and the question is (offset + count) mod n — so a subject walks its whole shelf before repeating any of it, starting from a per-campaign offset so two seeds do not hear the same twelve questions in the same order.

Two properties had to survive. The count advances inside the guard that already makes the body run once a session, so re-rendering cannot move it — the same rule that keeps a die out of this path. And it rides the save, so a reload resumes mid-shelf; a campaign saved before the rotation existed has no counter at all, loads, and starts clean.

The hash still picks which subject comes up, so it was rebuilt too — avalanched through Math.imul, which is an exact 32-bit multiply and therefore identical in every engine.

Seven readings, fifty-one defects

What the mechanical check could not see

The build already refused a question whose placeholders its subject cannot supply, or whose tone the engine cannot map. None of that catches a question that is simply another question wearing different nouns. Two rounds of adversarial readers — reskin, voice, fit, and for the second round sibling collision — read all seventy against the pool already shipped, each finding required to arrive with ready-to-paste replacement text.

Sibling collision · blocking · ten items

Two agents write each subject — one for the government benches, one for the opposition — and neither can see the other. Four pairs chose the same id, and two more collided under different ones: both had independently written “name the figure at which you would stop” and “your written answers come back with the same stock phrase; give me a date.”

Fit · blocking

{number} standing as the bare subject of a sentence in a question whose body never introduced a figure. Substituted, the line read: “23 stopped being an opinion about relations and became a description of them.”

Voice · blocking

A result line addressing the player as “you” — the only second person anywhere in the batch. Vale is written in the third person and the past tense: the House noted, recorded, did the arithmetic.

Voice · minor

A mobile telephone, in a corpus that otherwise runs on front pages, leaflets, minute books, warrants and the library. An invented verb. A duck that assessed the answer instead of naming a consequence. A spaced hyphen where the rest of the pool uses an em dash.

Twenty-two fixes were applied in the second round and five deliberately were not — four because the item they targeted was replaced whole, one because a second reader rewrote the same reply better. Each of those is named in the applier with its reason rather than dropped quietly.

Seven ways of asking, on every subject

The pool

A subject is only offered when its facts exist — a question about a governor needs a governor's ballot in sight, a question about a work needs one in trouble. The five levelled last are the ones that fire most often, because the state they need almost always exists.

Filled marks are questions put to a government you lead; hollow marks are questions you put from the opposition benches.

The third this order

The test guarding all of this was checking nothing. It cleared q.v10 — the guard that provides the stability — and then asked whether a forced re-selection re-selected. It now calls v8EnsureQuestion fifty times with the guard untouched, as fifty renders would; holds a subject to prove it walks its whole shelf; and strips the counter from a save to prove a campaign written before the rotation still loads and asks. It is proved red by three separate one-line mutations.
